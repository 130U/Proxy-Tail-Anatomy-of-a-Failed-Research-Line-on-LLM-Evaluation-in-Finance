# Parallel survey questions Q1–Q32

## How to use

- **The 32 questions are independent of each other** and have no order; they can all be sent out at once or in any batches.
- Open one task per question. At the start of the task, first read `00_CODEX_BRIEF.md`, `01_ADVISOR_MEMO.md`, `02_PROJECT_SNAPSHOT.md`, and the sections of `04_THEORY_NOTES.md` for the T numbers mentioned in the question.
- **Internet access is required.** Do not answer without internet access.
- Write the output to `research/answers/Qxx.md`, in the format of section 1 of `07_REPORTING_PROTOCOL.md`. 800–1500 characters per question is about right, with literature covered up to November 2026.
- The paper IDs mentioned in the questions come from the advisor's abstract-level searches and **may be wrong**. Rely on primary sources; if you find that an ID or description is wrong, point it out.
- **Overturning the advisor's hypothesis is as valuable as confirming it.**

**Five things every question must answer:**

1. Conclusion
2. Evidence table (label every number with its source tier)
3. Novelty judgment: already done / partly done / not found, with search keywords and confidence
4. Minimal viable check: a draft theorem statement, or an experiment design that runs within a few hours on a T4
5. Kill condition: what finding would make us abandon this direction

**Q2. A complete taxonomy of leakage channels.**

For each channel, answer: has any paper measured its magnitude? Which channels have never been measured at all? Compare with T1.1 in `04_THEORY_NOTES.md` to see whether the definition covers all channels.

**Q9. Placing semantic permissions in theory.** (corresponds to T2.1)

Map the repository's semantic permissions (four relations, three loss terms; see section 3.1 of `02_PROJECT_SNAPSHOT.md`) onto existing theory: counterfactual invariance (Veitch et al. 2021), counterfactually augmented data (Kaushik et al. 2020) and theoretical explanations of its effectiveness, contrast sets (Gardner et al. 2020), CheckList, ARTS, counterfactual logit pairing (Garg et al. 2019), consistency regularization (UDA, R-Drop), IRM, content–style identifiability (von Kügelgen et al. 2021; 2311.08815), group-equivariant learning.

3. What is the closest existing work to the "margin on the log-odds difference" loss?

**Q10. Certifiable monotonicity and "the model's Greeks".** (corresponds to T2.2)

Summarize the work on certifiable monotone networks: Liu et al. 2020 "Certified Monotonic Neural Networks"; the Lipschitz monotone networks of Kitouni–Nolte–Williams (2112.00038, 2307.07512) and their application in the LHCb Run 3 trigger system (2312.14265).

**Q11. Entity neutrality and exact invariance.** (corresponds to T2.4)

## Mathematical progress and the strict comparison target

[IA1–IA8](../theory/impact_audit.md) give a structural impact contract for arbitrary joint deletions, monotonicity of bad configurations, a reduction from minimal bad document sets to antichains of layer counts, the global minimum cost for a fixed design, the strongest joint zero-finding rectangle procedure, and an exact search over symmetric threshold families.

An independent discussion corrected an important choice of baseline. If a procedure reports the contamination count box only when every layer has zero findings, its exact worst-case miscoverage probability is the maximum over layers of the probability that an out-of-bound layer goes undetected. Using the Bonferroni sum as the strongest baseline would overstate the advantage. The main comparison in this round therefore uses the stronger max version, and states explicitly that its extra commitment is that the whole box itself is covered. Procedures that guarantee only the final statistical interval belong to the direct class. This round cannot claim to beat every possible confidence procedure.

The [general checker](../planning/impact_audit.py) fully enumerates any declared impact graph with at most 16 documents. It supports accuracy and fixed-class macro-F1, uses rational numbers, and refuses to issue a certificate when the budget is exceeded. The [symmetric checker](../planning/threshold_design.py) uses the proved reductions to handle larger structural families. It does not claim that general graphs scale the same way.

## Numerical results and negative results

Before running, the full grid of 20 cells was fixed: r∈{2,3,4,5,6} and N∈{2,5,10,20} per layer, with alpha=.05 and a target accuracy interval of [.9,1]. All votes are hand-built structures that are unanimously correct; they are not trained models. The raw output and the full table are in [impact_report](../planning/impact_report.md) and [threshold_results.json](../planning/threshold_results.json).

- For r=6, 11 layers and 20 documents per layer: the direct global optimum is 88, the strongest rectangle is 114, and the sum rectangle is 119. The saving in the main comparison is 13/57≈22.81%.
- The largest single-cell saving is 26.67%. 9 cells improve strictly and 11 do not improve. The median saving over the full 20 cells is 0, and only 3 cells exceed 20%. **The pre-registered family as a whole failed the proposal gate of a 20% median saving.**
- In the two-layer AND negative control, the direct optimum and the rectangle optimum are both 19. This refutes "joint structure always lowers the optimal cost".
- An existing analytic growth family gives an Ω(r) lower bound on the rectangle/direct cost ratio. It grows with r overall, and it is a hand-built oracle structure. It is the price of the extra rectangle commitment and is not presented as a conclusion about practical utility or originality.

The most important result of this round is not picking out the largest percentage. It is a method difference that is provable, executable and fairly qualified, together with a demonstration of which parameters give no gain. The next step is to find out whether the same structure appears in real training impact graphs, and whether the existing audit literature already covers this difference.

## Real sources: what evidence was strengthened

The [source audit report](../data/source_audit.md) selected 10 of the 120 local FOMC documents by a fixed date rule, and read the corresponding minutes and 8 saved official index/calendar pages. For all 10, the Released date next to the link matches the manifest, and the snapshot hash matches the full-text hash. The gap from meeting date to release date is 20–21 days. The dates were not chosen by random sampling, so 10/10 cannot be interpreted as an upper bound on the population mislabel rate.

The independent review found that the date parser could assign to the target link the only date in a paragraph, even when that date sat next to a different document. After the fix, all known minutes links in the container must belong to the same document identity. Only HTML/PDF aliases of that minutes document are allowed, and different identities or unknown links are rejected. A counterexample test was added and the fixed 10 documents were re-audited; the results still agree. This closes an implementation ambiguity, not the question of historical authenticity.

It is still unknown whether the bytes fetched in 2026 were available unchanged in the historical years. The release dates are precise only to the day, and the historical selection process is not certified either. So at present we may say "date claims supported by locally saved official sources", but not "the historical no-look-ahead core is certified".

The local source and approved scope of SEntFiN were also clarified further. The user's authorization for local research supports preparing a new private baseline for the original task, entity-level sentiment. The original labels cannot be renamed as investment stances on verified securities. Human confirmation does exist, but its scope cannot be extended to all original labels, rights and events. The next round can concretely build a new experiment and no longer needs to treat the missing old checkpoint as an obstacle. Training, splits and evaluation need a new version and verifiable identities of their own.

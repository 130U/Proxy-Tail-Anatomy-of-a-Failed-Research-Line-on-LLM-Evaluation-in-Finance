# Round 9: removing the fixed-quota restriction, simple random whole-stratum audits are conditionally optimal

Date: 2026-11-16. This round continued the derivation on the design-class restriction exposed in round 8; no network access, external contact, model training or real source audit. Fable is the advisor designated by the user; this document is for the user to pass on and has not been sent; the independent review comes from an internal AI and is not Fable's opinion.

## 1. Substantive change to the topic assessment

**In the homogeneous r-of-d threat structure, the restriction of pre-fixing per-stratum quotas can be removed, giving an exact lower bound for any randomized audit and a simple strategy that attains it.** The optimal strategy is to census several randomly chosen whole strata and then spot-check the remainder in one further random stratum. This is more complete than only proving that some optimizer beats one fixed baseline, and it further weakens the case that "a complex audit optimizer is needed".

The [full proof](../theory/randomized_audit.md) and the [independent review](randomization_review.md) support a narrow mathematical conclusion: the influence structure and the allowed sampling randomness together determine audit cost. It does not prove that training a language model from scratch is necessary, does not certify real historical sources, and has not been checked for novelty. The original G0/G1 and the full project terminal state are still not reached.

## 2. Conditions the main theorem must carry

There are d strata, each with N documents, all at the same cost. The threat family is exactly the set of configurations in which at least r strata each contain one bad document, 1≤r≤d. Audit verdicts are correct; the true bad set is fixed before random sampling. Any distribution may be used to draw m distinct documents, including correlated sampling and random per-stratum quotas.

The output convention is that **when every audit result is zero, the pre-specified interval I must be reported; if anything is found, the full range is reported**. If one says only "a certificate may be issued only on zero findings", the system can refuse to issue a certificate forever at zero cost, and the so-called optimal error risk degenerates to 0. Our optimality does not cover such arbitrary abstaining procedures.

If r-of-d is only an outer envelope of the actual model's danger set, the theorem optimizes only this conservative threat family; the real model might allow cheaper certificates. If the bad set can change after seeing the sample, labels can be wrong, or stratum sizes or costs differ, this formula cannot be applied directly.

Let q=⌊m/N⌋, t=m−qN. When m<dN, the minimax probability of issuing an erroneous certificate within the restricted class is

$$
V(m)=\frac{\binom{d-q-1}{r}+(1-t/N)\binom{d-q-1}{r-1}}{\binom{d}{r}}.
$$

Invalid binomial coefficients are taken as 0; m=dN is listed separately with V=0. Randomly choosing q whole strata and then sampling t documents uniformly in one further random stratum attains this value. For r=1 the optimum is usually not unique; concentrated quotas are not universally strictly better than spread-out ones.

The two halves of the proof are as follows. A uniform prior over minimal bad sets gives a lower bound on the average risk of any audit design; pairwise mass transfers on the elementary symmetric polynomials of the unaudited fractions concentrate quotas into whole strata and at most one partial stratum without increasing the average risk. Then random permutations of strata and of documents within strata make the miss risk equal for every fixed minimal bad set, attaining the lower bound. The average risk of a single deterministic set of whole strata is not mislabelled as the worst-case risk.

Adaptive choices along the all-zero path can be unrolled in advance into a randomized scheme, so with the same rule that all-zero must issue a certificate, binary ground-truth feedback only, and a hard budget, there is no better risk. If random sample sizes are allowed, the linear interpolation of V is convex; the expected budget must constrain the cost in the all-clean world or the cost under every ground truth. Constraining only the expected cost in bad worlds fails: with a single stratum of 2 documents, 1 of them bad, checking in random order until something is found gives zero risk at an average of 1.5 documents in the bad world, while the all-clean world needs 2. This early-stopping example cannot be used to claim a breach of the main lower bound.

## 3. Giving the total-count gate equally strong sampling power

To certify that the total number of bad documents K≤r−1, allowing the same arbitrary randomized m-document designs, the optimal zero-finding risk is

$$
V_K(m)=\frac{\binom{dN-m}{r}}{\binom{dN}{r}}.
$$

A uniform prior over bad sets of exactly r documents gives the lower bound, and uniform sampling without replacement over the whole population (pooled SRS) attains it. So the pooled baseline in the new table is not just a convenient-to-implement scheme; it is also the optimal randomized audit strategy under this stronger total-count commitment. Both procedures allow random quotas; the difference comes from which bad sets must be ruled out, not from one-sidedly relaxing the algorithm's capabilities.

## 4. Full 32-cell results and the zero gains that must be kept

The [execution protocol](../planning/RANDOMIZATION_PROTOCOL.md) was written before the run, but after the old results had been seen and candidate formulas discussed, so it is explicitly exploratory. All inputs are hand-made mathematical structures. α=1/20; all 20 old thresholds, 8 all-strata conjunctions and 4 sharp paths are reported. The [full table](../planning/randomization_table.md) and [exact JSON](../planning/randomization_results.json) store the risk at the current size and at one document fewer. The 16 star structures have asymmetric roles, are outside this theorem's scope, and are not mixed into the success rate.

| Family | Cells | Strictly fewer samples than optimal pooled | Strictly fewer samples than the fixed-direct cost lower bound | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Original threshold family | 20 | 20 | 18 | Median saving vs pooled 64/315, about 20.32%; not a retroactive pass of the original 20% gate |
| All-strata conjunction | 8 | 8 | 0 | Fixed auditing of one full stratum is already optimal; randomization adds no gain |
| Sharp paths | 4 | 4 | 3 | Fixed quotas keep only the original upper and lower bounds; for N=4 the randomized cost equals that lower bound, so no strict improvement can be claimed |

Representative sharp case N=32, d=2047, r=1024, 65504 documents in total:

| Method and its commitment | Minimum audit size or proven interval |
|---|---:|
| Any randomized audit, ruling out r damaged strata | **141 (exact optimum)** |
| Any randomized audit, certifying K≤1023; attained by pooled | **190 (exact optimum)** |
| Direct with fixed per-stratum quotas | [1024,1118], still a bound |
| Fixed quotas min(Box,TotalK) | [2016,2047], still a bound |

The 141-document strategy audits 32 documents in each of 4 random strata, then 13 documents in one further random stratum. Its risk is about 0.04953064; at 140 documents the risk is about 0.05050482, so one cannot go one document lower. Relative to optimal pooled, the saving is 49/190, about 25.79%; relative to the fixed-direct cost lower bound, the saving is at least 883/1024, about 86.23%. The latter reflects the effect of relaxing the fixed-quota restriction and must not be mixed up with a pure structural-modelling gain. Round 8's 192 documents was only a feasible pooled size; this round's 190 is the exact minimum.

So one cannot just highlight that all 32 cells beat pooled: the conjunction family already has an equally good fixed single-stratum clean gate; all these positive results are attained by simple strategies and do not prove that a complex architecture is necessary.

## 5. Implementation and independent review

The [exact kernel](../planning/randomized_audit.py) computes risks with integers and Fraction, finds minimum sample sizes by bisection, and provides a reproducible strategy sampler. A fixed seed is used for replay; a single seed known in advance is not by itself a repeated-sampling guarantee. The implementation does not claim to provide adaptive or expected-budget algorithms; those two items are theoretical extensions.

The first 32-cell run took 0.643217 seconds; the run in the final integration took 0.651663 seconds. The pre-set budget was 30 seconds; peak memory was not measured. The final unified reproduction had **125 unit tests pass and all 13 subcommands exit 0**, plus 10 source-parsing self-tests; real model training was not repeated. The [reproduction log](../reproduction_log.json) keeps the actual commands and outputs; after the timestamp fields of old research results were refreshed, the new table is bound to the current input hash.

The independent review does not import the author's kernel: for all 27 (d,N) pairs with dN≤10, 87 (d,N,r) triples and 709 budget cells, it directly enumerates audited documents and minimal bad sets. The uniform-prior lower bound, the worst-case risk of the explicit strategy, and the formula are all equal; convexity of the 87 risk curves also holds. Generality rests on the pen-and-paper proof; finite enumeration is not machine formal verification. The main table is additionally checked cell by cell for exact risk and for infeasibility at one document fewer; details and the final hashes are in the [review record](randomization_review.md).

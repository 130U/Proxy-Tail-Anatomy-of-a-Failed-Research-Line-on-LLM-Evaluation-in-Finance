# Round 8: the value of localization has strict limits, and the stronger simple audit must be kept

Date: 2026-11-16. The previous round completed the consolidated draft and the adversarial review. This round continues to test the most critical necessity question in it. Everything was local: no network access, no model training, no real-source audit, and nothing sent to Fable. G0/G1 and the project as a whole have still not passed.

## 1. What judgments this round changed

**Localization structure has provable value, but that value depends on which audit designs are allowed, and it cannot be used to claim that a complex optimizer is already necessary.** The [complete conditional proof](../theory/localization_value.md) gives a general risk algorithm, conditions under which there is no gain, an upper bound on the cost ratio for homogeneous threshold families, and constructions that attain it. The [independent adversarial review](localization_review.md) checks both the mathematics and the strongest simple alternative.

The 20 cells were originally compared against a single rectangle. Once the optimal total-contamination-count gate is added, only 5 cells still improve strictly over the simple combination that picks the better of the two ex ante. The largest saving is 1/11, about 9.09%, and the median is 0. The new conjunction and star structures can gain an asymptotic order relative to the total-count gate, but a single-layer clean rectangle already attains the direct optimum. One cannot pick, for each structure, only the baseline that is least favorable to it.

Another parameter sequence keeps a positive mathematical result: within the fixed-quota class, the cost ratio between the direct method and this simple combination can approach 2. But if random sampling over the whole population is allowed, a new simple scheme may again be cheaper. This restriction on the design class is now the central question in deciding whether this direction is worth continuing. It is not a footnote.

## 2. What has been proved

| Result | Conditions and meaning |
|---|---|
| Exact risk of the total-count gate | When the minimum number of bad documents is s, the largest safe upper bound on the total count is s−1. The per-layer miss probability decomposes into a chain of non-increasing factors, and the product of the s largest factors over all chains is the exact risk. Applies to unequal layer sizes; no IID training assumption. |
| Finite optimal quotas | Exact search over a general finite grid. With equal sizes, equal costs and s≤d, one can prove a reduction that keeps only the s smallest quotas. The actual implementation keeps the full d-dimensional ordering and prunes with a safe lower bound; it does not falsely claim to implement the new reduction. |
| Cases with no localization value | With single-layer uniform sampling without replacement, the direct method and the optimal total-count gate are exactly equivalent. A finer impact graph by itself gives no gain. |
| Homogeneous r-of-d upper bound | Optimal total-count cost / direct cost ≤ d/(d−r+1). For the original d=2r−1 family the ratio is below 2, and the constant 2 can be approached. |
| The two scalings go in opposite directions | In the original paper's N=r² sequence, the total-count/direct ratio tends to 1. In the new sharp sequence r=N² it can tend to 2. The two scalings cannot be swapped to merge the narratives. |
| Conjunction and star | Every document takes part in some minimal bad configuration, which rules out padding with irrelevant documents. A single-layer clean gate still attains the direct optimum, so complex graph optimization is not needed to obtain the gains in these examples. |

All of the above are conditional pen-and-paper proofs, not machine-checked formal verification, and no claim is made on this basis that novelty has been verified. The input impact graph, the real audit labels and the historical comparator are still premises.

## 4. A broader simple alternative: random stratum quotas change the conclusion

The class above requires the audit count of each layer to be fixed in advance. If all documents have equal cost and are all auditable, and we allow drawing n documents uniformly without replacement from M in total, then the per-layer quotas become random. The zero-finding risk when excluding a total contamination count of at least s is

$$
\frac{\binom{M-s}{n}}{\binom{M}{n}}
\le (1-s/M)^n\le \exp(-ns/M).
$$

When n>M−s the risk is exactly 0; otherwise the product bound above holds. Taking n large enough, but no larger than M, gives a total-count gate. On the sharp path with M=(2r−1)N and s=r=N², the required n is O(N), below the Θ(N²) cost of the fixed-quota class on this path.

A separate [exact arithmetic check](../planning/pooled_scope_check.json) uses N=32, M=65504, s=1024. When 192 documents are drawn from the whole population, the ratio of binomial coefficients is indeed ≤1/20, while the lower bound for the fixed-quota direct optimum is already 1024. 192 is only a feasible number and is not claimed to be the global optimum. This check was added explicitly after the review as a statement of the design-class boundary. It is not mixed into the original 48 rows or into the fixed-quota main comparison.

This does not refute the fixed-quota theorem, but it weakens the case for upgrading that theorem into a general algorithmic advantage. **If the real problem has no exogenous, explicit fixed-quota constraint, this broader and simpler random-sampling scheme must be kept; it cannot be excluded just to preserve the gain.** No such real constraint has been established so far.

## 5. Implementation, failures and review

The new [total-count gate implementation](../planning/total_count_design.py) provides an exact Fraction greedy algorithm, an independent binomial-coefficient DP and a finite optimal design search. Each search has cumulative budgets for nodes, factors and DP; when a budget is exceeded it does not return a false optimum. Inputs in exponent notation are rejected before parsing, so that short strings cannot trigger huge integers.

The first complete run of the 48 rows took about 1.476 seconds. In the first integration rerun, Windows refused the atomic replacement of the results file. 11 commands succeeded and the 12th failed, and the partial result was not called complete. The [failure snapshot](../planning/localization_io_failure.json) keeps the original error and the affected cases. The fix retries a bounded number of times, and only for whitelisted Windows sharing errors, with a cumulative wait of 0.775 seconds. A persistent failure still raises an error and keeps the old committed state. 3 new regression tests on real temporary files verify success, persistent failure, and that other errors are not swallowed.

After the fix, the 13 targeted tests and the rerun of the 48 rows pass. The final unified reproduction gives **119 unit tests passed and all 12 commands successful**, plus 10 source-parsing self-tests. The original model training was not rerun. The [reproduction record](../reproduction_log.json) and the [instructions](../REPRODUCE.md) register the current commands. The timings are observations on this machine, not theoretical complexity. Peak memory was not measured.

The independent review used programs that do not call the author's kernel: 900 fixed designs and 6788 risk comparisons, plus 309349 sorted prefixes enumerated over all 20 cells. The star, conjunction and sharp rows were checked separately by independent two-variable enumeration, closed forms and analytic bounds. Finite checks support the consistency of the implementation; they do not replace a general proof. Details and version hashes are in the [review report](localization_review.md).

## 6. Needs a ruling from Fable

This round's mathematical work shows **when aggregating counts loses useful structure, and when changing the sampling scheme is enough to solve the problem**. It makes the research question clearer, but it still does not prove that a new language model or a complex audit algorithm is irreplaceable.

Please first rule on whether the real research object has a reason that requires fixing the per-layer quotas. If not, this line should be kept as conditional theory and a baseline tool for P1, and new model training should not be started on the strength of the current constructions alone. If so, first write down that exogenous constraint, the real impact structure and the strong alternatives, and only then decide which narrow theorem is worth a novelty check. This round does not replace the original "must train from scratch" filter, the missing G0 evidence or the real-world non-emptiness requirement of G1.
